Compensation Design

The Cost of Compressed Compensation Bands in Scale-Ups

Analyzing why misaligned pay architectures trigger silent engineer turnover post-funding, and how to structure transparent salary bands.

What is Compensation Compression?

When early-stage startups hire their first developers, salaries are frequently set based on ad-hoc negotiations or whatever cash was available. As a startup raises a Series A or B round, hiring managers must scale recruitment efforts rapidly, paying current market rates to attract top talent. This creates "compression"—where new hires earn equal to or more than early-stage staff with equivalent experience.

This compression triggers quiet churn. Early employees feel undervalued, resulting in low morale and, eventually, resignations. Because startup feedback channels are often informal, leadership teams rarely recognize the true trigger for these departures.

The Solution: A 3-Step Re-alignment Plan

1.

Standardize Career Grade Levels

Map every role in your company to a specific grade tier (e.g. Engineer I, II, Senior, Lead). Ensure that leveling is based on objective skill benchmarks rather than individual negotiation capacity.

2.

Build Structured Salary Ranges

Define minimum, midpoint, and maximum salary parameters for each level, referencing local or remote market databases. Keep bands wide enough to allow for merit raises without changing grades.

3.

Transition Safely with Equity Incentives

Where cash limits prevent immediate salary corrections, adjust early-employee equity options to compensate for low base salaries, linking options to milestone vesting schedules.
